Dated cards at the top

Cards with a date (an event, an end date or a start date) get a “when” chip and a place at the top of the grid. This page compares four layouts and the chip's wording. Made 2026-11-19.

Decided 2026-11-19

Layout B: a “Coming up” section of full-size tiles above the grid, without repeating those cards below, plus the start date (“Valid from”). Built as described here, except that .pkpass files have no start-date field.

Recommendation

A: a “Coming up” strip above the A–Z grid. It is one row of small tiles, soonest first, each with a chip such as Today 20:30 Fri 07:05 Ends Sun From 1 Dec. The same chip also appears on those cards' tiles in the grid. The A–Z order stays intact and costs one row of height. The Now Bar stays at the bottom for what's due now, in thumb reach and with live flight info. Add a start date (“valid from”) for vouchers, gift cards, memberships and tickets, so “From 1 Dec” is possible.

What happens today

  • Stored: the event date and time for tickets and flights, and the end date for vouchers, gift cards and memberships. There is no start date.
  • Shown: the date appears on the card's code screen. The Now Bar at the bottom shows only today and tomorrow (an event today, a flight within 24 hours, a voucher ending today or tomorrow). Grid tiles show no date.
  • After the date: a past event or an expired card moves to the archive by itself.

Four layouts

Each mock-up shows the top of the grid with the same cards: a concert tonight, a flight on Friday, a voucher ending Sunday, a gym membership starting on 1 December, and shop cards without dates.

best balance It takes little height, the A–Z order stays intact, and you see what's coming at a glance. The cards appear twice (strip and grid), the same way favourites do in the rail.

tall Big targets with no duplicates, but four dated cards take two rows, and with eight of them the A–Z grid drops below the screen.

moves This is the simplest to build, but the order is no longer plain A–Z, and tiles shift as dates come and go, which is what A–Z was meant to stop.

most readable Full names and clear groups, but it reads like a list rather than cards, and it grows one line per dated card. It fits better as a sheet behind a “Coming up” button than at the top of the grid.

The “when” chip

The chip is short, relative while the date is close and a plain date once it's further away. Its colour says how urgent it is. It is used in the strip, on grid tiles and in the favourites rail.

Rules

  • Which cards: events from today on; end dates within 30 days; start dates within 30 days. Archived cards never appear.
  • Order: by the date that matters next: the event, the start date, or the end date.
  • Groups: two tickets for one show, or two passengers on one flight, stay one tile.
  • Search: the strip hides while you search, like the rail and the Now Bar.
  • Now Bar: stays at the bottom for what's due today or tomorrow. The strip is for looking ahead, so a concert tonight appears in both.
  • “Most used” order: the strip shows the same way; it doesn't depend on the grid's order.

Start dates (“valid from”)

Some vouchers and memberships only work from a certain day. This needs one new optional field, the same kind of change as the barcode switch: a database upgrade that only adds a column, so stored cards are untouched. Here's where it would show up:

  • Form: “Valid from” next to “Expires” for vouchers, gift cards, memberships and tickets.
  • Before the date: a “From 1 Dec” chip, and a “Valid from 1 Dec” line on the code screen. The code still shows; Wallet doesn't hide it.
  • On the day: Up next and the notification say “Valid from today”.
  • Import: ticket and voucher text with “geldig vanaf” or “valid from” fills it in. Apple Wallet passes have no start-date field.

Optional extra: filter chips

A row of chips at the top, AllComing upEnding soon, could filter the grid instead. It answers “what's coming?” with one tap, but it's another control at the top of the screen, where the one-handed design keeps things out. I'd skip it unless the strip turns out not to be enough.
