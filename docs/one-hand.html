One-handed layout and tile styles

Recommendation: A, Bottom-up, with D's bottom sheets and C's swipe-down to close. The cards you use most move to the bottom-right, next to the thumb; every action leaves the top of the screen; menus, settings and confirmations open as sheets from the bottom; a code closes with a swipe down anywhere or a large Done button. There is no one-handed mode to switch on: this is the only layout, and it also works with two hands.

Where the thumb reaches today

On a 6.7-inch phone held in the right hand, the thumb reaches the bottom and the middle-right easily, the middle-left with a stretch, and the top corners only by shifting the grip. The overlay shows this on the current screens; red markers are controls in the hard zone, green ones are fine.

Principles for every option

  1. Every frequent action lives in the bottom third; the top quarter holds only information.
  2. Leaving a screen never needs the top-left corner: swipe down, a bottom Done or Cancel, or the system Back gesture.
  3. Menus, confirmations and secondary screens rise from the bottom as sheets with full-width rows.
  4. The cards used most are the ones closest to the thumb.
  5. Targets are at least 48 dp, and whole rows are tappable, not just their switches.
  6. No one-handed mode or setting: the default layout is the one-handed layout.

Options

A. Bottom-up

The grid fills from the bottom. The most-used card sits bottom-right next to the thumb; less used cards rise towards the top.

StrengthsWeaknesses
  • Every frequent action is in the easy zone with no extra UI.
  • Uses the whole screen: the top holds rarely used cards, not empty space.
  • Small change to build: the grid is laid out bottom-up.
  • Reading order is unusual at first (most used at the bottom).
  • With few cards the top of the screen is empty.

Effort: Low.

B. Thumb dock

A dock in the bottom third holds Up next, the four favourite cards as large tiles, and search. The full grid scrolls above it.

StrengthsWeaknesses
  • The four cards used most are always under the thumb, even while scrolling.
  • Familiar top-to-bottom order for the rest of the grid.
  • The dock takes about a third of the screen, so fewer other cards show.
  • Favourites appear twice when the grid is scrolled to the top.

Effort: Medium.

C. Gestures

Big gestures anywhere on the screen replace top buttons: pull down to search, swipe up on the bar to fan out the top cards, swipe down to close a code.

StrengthsWeaknesses
  • Fastest once learned: open a card with one swipe and release.
  • Nothing has to be reached; the whole screen is the target.
  • Hidden: gestures need a hint the first time.
  • Must stay clear of One UI's edge gestures for Back and Home.

Effort: High.

D. Bottom sheets

Keep today's grid, but every top control moves down: Archive and Settings join the bottom bar, and menus, dialogs, settings and the archive open as sheets from the bottom.

StrengthsWeaknesses
  • Closest to the current app; nothing to relearn.
  • Sheets close with a swipe down from where the thumb already is.
  • Most-used cards stay at the top of the grid, out of easy reach.
  • Bottom bar gets crowded on narrow phones.

Effort: Low.

Tile styles

Five ways to make tiles more than a flat colour. All keep the brand colour as the main signal, keep text readable (white or near-black by contrast), and work in light and dark mode. The app has no brand logos, so each style works with colour, type, generated shapes and the card's own data. Recommendation: 5, Smart tile, with the mesh gradient of 2 as its background, because it adds information you would otherwise open the card for; 1 is the best purely visual choice.

1. Generated pattern

Each card gets a quiet pattern (stripes, dots, rings or waves) picked from its name, in a tone of its own colour. Same card, same pattern on every device.
